The importance of volume

Why the Volume Score and RVOL matter

Price tells you where a market is going; volume tells you how much conviction is behind the move. A breakout on heavy volume is far more trustworthy than the same breakout on a quiet day. This guide explains the volume measures you will see across the platform.

01

Relative volume (RVOL)

RVOL compares today's trading volume against what is normal for that instrument. It is a ratio, so it is comparable across a small share and a large index:

BandWhat it means
RVOL ≥ 2A genuine volume spike — roughly twice normal or more. The strongest confirmation.
RVOL ≥ 1Above normal — more interest than usual, worth noting.
RVOL < 1Thin — below normal participation. Treat a breakout here with more caution.

These are the same bands used everywhere RVOL appears, so a value reads the same way on the Scanner, on Instruments and in the Performance analysis.

02

The Volume Score

The Volume Score (0–12) is a single breakout-confirmation number that folds several volume-and-momentum signals together — is volume expanding, is price holding above its volume-weighted average price (VWAP), is the average true range (ATR) widening. A higher score means the breakout has more genuine participation behind it.

Because it is one number, the Volume Score is an easy tie-breaker: where two setups look similar on price, the one with more volume behind it is the more reliable.

03

VWAP and ATR

  • VWAP (volume-weighted average price) — the average price weighted by where the volume actually traded. Price holding above VWAP on a bullish setup says buyers are in control.
  • ATR (average true range) — a measure of how much the instrument moves in a session. An expanding ATR at the breakout confirms the spring is genuinely releasing energy, not drifting.
Volume figures confirm or caution — they do not override the pattern. A clean squeeze on strong volume is the ideal; a clean squeeze on thin volume is a reason to size carefully.
